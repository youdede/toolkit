# Qualify Co-Marketing Partner

## Contract

A partner must create credible value for both audiences, not only borrowed reach. Use public and supplied evidence, disclose uncertainty, and separate strategic fit from personal familiarity.

## Score fit and shape the agreement

1. Define campaign objective, audience need, non-negotiables, brand risks, capacity, timing, and the contribution the requesting organization can genuinely provide.
2. Research audience overlap, complementary value, reputation, content quality, distribution behavior, prior partnerships, competitive conflicts, data practices, and decision ownership.
3. Score fit with explicit evidence and disqualifiers. Design a smallest viable campaign with balanced inputs, approvals, lead handling, accessibility, attribution, and cancellation terms.
4. Prepare outreach around mutual audience value and a concrete low-friction next step. After agreement, document owners, assets, dates, rights, metrics, and a post-campaign review.

## Validation

- The partner score cites observable evidence and identifies unknowns requiring conversation.
- The proposal states reciprocal contributions, audience value, ownership, data handling, and exit conditions.
- Measurement distinguishes reach, qualified engagement, pipeline, and relationship value.

## Stop conditions

Stop when reputation concerns, audience harm, competitive conflicts, data sharing, content rights, or unequal commitments cannot be resolved before outreach.

## Refusals

- It does not scrape private contacts, misrepresent an existing relationship, or imply endorsement before agreement.
- It does not trade audience data or add contacts to marketing systems without appropriate consent.
- It does not recommend a partner solely because of follower count or brand prestige.
