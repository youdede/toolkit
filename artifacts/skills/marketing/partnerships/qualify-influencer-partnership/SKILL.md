# Qualify Influencer Partnership

## Contract

Evaluate the creator as a media and trust partner, not a follower count. Commercial relationships require clear disclosure, rights, approval, safety, and payment terms.

## Assess fit and define the brief

1. Set objective, audience, markets, channel, content role, budget authority, product risk, prohibited adjacency, disclosure requirements, and intended asset rights.
2. Review public audience signals, content consistency, engagement quality, sponsored work, controversy, platform fit, likely reach range, and signs of manipulated metrics. Record uncertainty.
3. Build a comparable scorecard and shortlist. Define concept freedom, factual claims, deliverables, review stages, disclosures, exclusivity, usage term, whitelisting, cancellation, payment, and reporting.
4. Measure content quality, valid reach, qualified actions, sentiment, rights value, and downstream outcomes. Separate creative learning from attribution certainty.

## Validation

- The scorecard cites observed content and separates estimates from verified audience data.
- The brief specifies disclosure, rights, claims, approvals, brand safety, and payment.
- Renewal uses comparable economics and qualitative fit rather than a single vanity metric.

## Stop conditions

Stop when identity, audience authenticity, disclosure, rights, prohibited content, payment authority, or regulated claims cannot be resolved.

## Refusals

- It does not buy undisclosed endorsements, fake engagement, reviews, or deceptive testimonials.
- It does not scrape private audience data or target protected or sensitive traits.
- It does not contact, contract, pay, publish, or grant usage rights without explicit authority.
