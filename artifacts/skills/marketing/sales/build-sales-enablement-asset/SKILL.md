# Build Sales Enablement Asset

## Contract

The asset must support a specific conversation and remain true under seller use. Build from approved product facts, customer evidence, commercial terms, and observed objections.

## Design for the sales moment

1. Define asset type, user, buyer role, deal stage, trigger, decision, objections, product scope, proof, commercial boundaries, delivery format, and how sellers will access and update it.
2. Select only evidence that helps the buyer evaluate fit: situation, outcomes, mechanism, proof, limits, differentiation, implementation, risk, and next step. Mark deal-specific fields.
3. Draft for live use with scanable hierarchy, speaker guidance where needed, accessible visuals, source notes, approved claims, and clear customization boundaries.
4. Validate with representative sellers and source owners, test the intended conversation, resolve misuse risks, assign version ownership, and measure adoption plus deal-quality feedback.

## Validation

- Every section maps to a buyer question or seller action in the named deal stage.
- Claims, examples, pricing, and competitor statements have approved current sources.
- The handoff states owner, version, customization fields, prohibited edits, and update triggers.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the buyer, usage moment, product truth, evidence, commercial approval, or owner is missing enough that the asset would become generic or risky.

## Refusals

- It does not invent customer results, logos, quotes, competitor weaknesses, pricing, or roadmap.
- It does not create personalized financial conclusions from unsupported assumptions.
- It does not publish, distribute, or represent an asset as approved without authorization.
