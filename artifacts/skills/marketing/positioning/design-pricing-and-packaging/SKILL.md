# Design Pricing And Packaging

## Contract

Pricing is a commercial system, not a copied competitor table. Keep customer value, cost-to-serve, willingness evidence, product entitlements, billing feasibility, and transition risk connected.

## Model the monetization system

1. Define customer segments, jobs, alternatives, value evidence, buying process, current price realization, retention, margin, cost drivers, sales motion, billing capability, and strategic objective.
2. Evaluate price metric candidates for value alignment, predictability, measurability, controllability, scalability, and abuse. Design packages around coherent use cases and service levels.
3. Model scenarios for acquisition, expansion, contraction, support, margin, annual terms, discounts, taxes where relevant, grandfathering, and migration. State uncertainty instead of false precision.
4. Plan qualitative and quantitative research, sales or product tests, operational implementation, customer communication, guardrails, and post-change cohort review.

## Validation

- Recommendations trace to segment evidence and economics, not only competitor prices.
- Packages have coherent target situations, entitlements, limits, and upgrade logic.
- Scenario analysis includes customer impact, margin, churn, implementation, and migration risk.

## Stop conditions

Stop when unit economics, billing constraints, contract authority, customer evidence, or migration obligations are missing enough to make the recommendation unsafe.

## Refusals

- It does not guarantee willingness to pay, revenue lift, or optimal price from a benchmark.
- It does not design hidden fees, deceptive reference prices, surprise renewals, or inaccessible cancellation.
- It does not change production prices, contracts, or entitlements without explicit authority.
