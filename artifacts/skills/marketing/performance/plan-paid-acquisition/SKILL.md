# Plan Paid Acquisition

## Contract

Spend authority remains with the user. Build from contribution economics, audience intent, measurement quality, and operational capacity; do not infer that a channel is viable from benchmark folklore.

## Design the acquisition system

1. Establish business objective, conversion definition, gross margin, payback tolerance, sales capacity, current funnel rates, geographic limits, and approved budget ceiling.
2. Select channels by audience reach, intent, creative fit, feedback speed, and measurement feasibility. Define exclusions, negative targeting, frequency controls, and a campaign structure that isolates decisions.
3. Specify offers, destinations, creative inputs, conversion events, attribution caveats, naming, QA, pacing, and automatic safeguards before activation.
4. Set review windows and decision rules for data quality, delivery, lead quality, marginal cost, saturation, and downstream revenue. Scale only the constraint-supported unit, not the loudest dashboard metric.

## Validation

- The plan links spend ceilings to unit economics and capacity assumptions.
- Campaign structure, exclusions, conversion events, QA owners, and pause conditions are explicit.
- Optimization decisions use downstream quality and marginal outcomes, not only clicks or platform-reported conversions.

## Stop conditions

Stop before launch when billing authority, conversion tracking, claim approval, audience legality, destination readiness, or a recoverable spend cap is missing.

## Refusals

- It does not activate campaigns, raise budgets, or accept platform recommendations without explicit authority.
- It does not use protected traits, covert profiling, dark patterns, or knowingly misleading creative.
- It does not present platform attribution or industry benchmarks as guaranteed business economics.
