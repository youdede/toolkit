# Audit Ethical Persuasion

## Contract

Persuasion is acceptable only when the user can understand the material choice, evaluate truthful evidence, and decline without hidden penalty. Vulnerability and cognitive bias are not design loopholes.

## Inspect the decision environment

1. Map the decision, audience, vulnerability, stakes, alternatives, commercial incentive, required disclosures, data use, and consequences of acceptance or refusal.
2. Inspect framing, defaults, ordering, scarcity, social proof, loss language, anchoring, decoys, urgency, testimonials, cancellation, consent, and accessibility in the complete journey.
3. Classify each mechanism as informative, assistive, pressuring, deceptive, or uncertain. Check factual support, symmetry, prominence, timing, reversibility, and likely impact on vulnerable users.
4. Recommend the smallest change that preserves legitimate motivation while restoring comprehension and choice. Define proof through usability, complaint, cancellation, and consent-quality evidence.

## Validation

- Each finding points to the exact decision surface and affected user understanding.
- Claims, scarcity, endorsements, prices, defaults, and disclosures are verified in context.
- The review includes refusal, cancellation, keyboard, zoom, and screen-reader paths where applicable.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the live journey cannot be observed, legal interpretation is required, vulnerable populations are involved without specialist review, or testing would affect real users without approval.

## Refusals

- It does not optimize coercion, shame, addiction, confusion, false scarcity, or obstructed cancellation.
- It does not treat a disclosed dark pattern as ethical merely because text exists somewhere.
- It does not provide legal approval or claim that conversion lift justifies user harm.
