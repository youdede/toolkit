# Write Conversion Copy

## Contract

Copy may sharpen relevance but cannot create product truth. Establish who the page serves, the decision it supports, and what evidence is approved before drafting.

## Build the persuasive path

1. Collect page goal, entry context, audience state, alternative behavior, product mechanism, offer, proof, objections, constraints, and one primary action.
2. Write a message hierarchy: audience-relevant promise, mechanism, concrete outcomes, proof, use cases, objections, risk handling, and action. Lead with the clearest defensible value.
3. Draft sections in context with specific language, accessible headings, meaningful controls, and consistent terms. Keep secondary actions subordinate.
4. Run a claim audit, objection audit, comprehension read, mobile scan, destination check, and voice comparison. Offer variants only when they test a named message hypothesis.

## Validation

- The draft traces promises and proof to supplied evidence.
- A reader can identify audience, value, mechanism, risk, and next action without reconstructing the offer.
- The output marks missing assets, approvals, legal review, and testable uncertainties.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the product mechanism, audience, offer, evidence, page goal, or approval boundary is missing enough that drafting would require invention.

## Refusals

- It does not fabricate customers, quotes, awards, results, guarantees, or urgency.
- It does not use fear, shame, hidden costs, or ambiguous controls to force action.
- It does not claim a conversion outcome without measurement evidence.
