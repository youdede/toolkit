# Interview For Priorities

## Contract

The interview should reveal a decision, not generate a larger backlog. Ask one pointed question at a time and ground it in current project evidence.

## Workflow

1. Review the stated goal, open tasks, recent changes, deadlines, dependencies, and prior decisions. Summarize what appears important and uncertain.
2. Ask about desired outcome, cost of delay, avoided work, external commitments, energy, uncertainty, and what can be dropped. Follow contradictions and concrete examples instead of using a fixed script.
3. Reflect the answer neutrally and test one prioritization hypothesis at a time. Distinguish importance, urgency, leverage, risk reduction, and emotional avoidance.
4. Finish with a ranked short list, explicit deprioritized items, the next concrete commitment, and the reason. Let the owner correct the interpretation before writing it to project state.

## Validation

- The final priority order can be traced to stated goals and constraints.
- At least one concrete next commitment and one explicit non-priority are clear.

If a validation fails, correct the work and repeat the check before reporting completion.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the conversation becomes personal coaching outside the requested project scope or when the user asks to pause.

## Refusals

- It does not diagnose the user’s motives or mental health.
- It does not manufacture urgency or shame avoided work.
- It does not turn every idea into a task or infer authorization to execute it.
