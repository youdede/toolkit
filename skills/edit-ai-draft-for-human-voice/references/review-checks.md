# Review checks for any text

Questions to ask while reading an article, a web page, a guide or an email for the editorial review. They come from a taxonomy of narrative features; only the ones that still mean something outside a story are kept. They carry no weights and give no score: a "yes" marks a passage worth quoting, not a verdict on who wrote the text. Every finding still quotes the text and changes nothing but wording.

Each line gives the question, then the `kind` a finding takes in `review.json`.

## Words and figures

- Are the metaphors stock phrases ("a game changer", "the tip of the iceberg") rather than images tied to this subject? `generic`
- Does an abstract noun stand where the text knows a concrete one ("solutions", "processes", "the landscape")? `generic`
- Does the vocabulary circle a few words, so the same adjective or verb returns paragraph after paragraph? `repetitive`
- Does the register shift without reason, from plain to formal or from technical to chatty? `tone`
- Is there jargon or in-group shorthand the intended reader would not know, left unexplained? `unclear`
- Does a long Latinate word replace a short plain one with the same meaning? `unclear`

## Sentences

- Do the sentences run at one length, so the rhythm never changes? `structure`
- Are clauses chained with "and", "while", "which" until the main point is buried? `unclear`
- Does a sentence pack several facts so that none of them lands? `unclear`
- Does a fragment or a one-line paragraph claim emphasis the content does not earn? `tone`
- Do parenthetical asides interrupt more than they add? `unclear`

## The writer's voice

- Does the text comment on its own content instead of giving it ("it is worth noting", "importantly", "let's dive in")? `generic`
- Does it judge where it should show: "remarkable", "crucial", "a substantial achievement"? `tone`
- Is the evaluative pitch higher than the evidence: every point "key", every change "significant"? `tone`
- Is the voice anonymous where the reader expects a person, or personal where they expect neutral facts? `tone`
- Does the text address the reader ("you might wonder") more often than it helps them? `tone`
- Does the person shift (I, we, you, one) without reason? `inconsistent`

## What the reader is assumed to know

- Does the text assume knowledge its reader lacks, or explain what they already know? `unclear`
- Does a name, a code, or an internal label stand for the reader ("the persona", "the scoped user")? `tone`
- Is one thing called by several names? `inconsistent`

## Claims and sources

- Is there a claim, number, or comparison with no source and no reasoning on the page? `unsupported`
- Does the text rely on one kind of source where the claim needs another? `unsupported`
- Do two passages give different numbers, names, or dates for the same thing? `inconsistent`
- Are dates and periods vague where the text could be exact ("recently", "in the coming months")? `generic`

## Shape of the whole

- Does the text answer the question its title or opening asks? `structure`
- Does it chase more themes than it can hold, or drift away from the one it set? `structure`
- Does a section restate an earlier one instead of adding to it? `repetitive`
- Does a heading promise something the section under it does not deliver? `inconsistent`
- Does it close on a moral, a lesson, or a summary of what it just said? `generic`
- Does a section state a need or a problem and stop before saying what was done? `structure`
- Does a contrast frame ("not X, but Y") recur until it reads as a tic? `repetitive`
